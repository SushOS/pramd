    02 — The problem

    Outside English the failure is representational, not gradual

    T5, the frozen text interface of the OMG backbone, maps Devanagari to unknown tokens at 43.6%. Measured on 1,000 distinct real captions per language, distinct sentences collapse onto shared token sequences — and identical token ids give identical hidden states and identical embeddings. Two different Hindi sentences can reach the generator as byte-identical input.

    Retrieval falls from R@1 0.317 in English to 0.133, and caption sensitivity to 0.3%. The motion still looks plausible, because the motion prior does not need the text to produce something humanoid — so distribution scores alone do not establish that the instruction was followed.

    03 — Method

    Output geometry, not output shape

    An Indic caption is encoded by frozen MuRIL, mapped by the trained adapter, and consumed by the frozen OMG denoiser. Nothing else receives a gradient.

    Writing \(h=\mathrm{MuRIL}(\text{caption})\in\mathbb{R}^{50\times 768}\), the adapter is a per-token residual MLP with a learned scalar gate \(\alpha\), followed by an affine recolouring:

    \[ u=\mathrm{LN}(h),\qquad r=W_2\,\mathrm{drop}\!\left(\mathrm{GELU}(W_1u)\right),\qquad y=u+\alpha\,r,\qquad \hat{c}=\frac{y-\mu_{\mathrm{src}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{src}}}\odot\gamma+\beta . \]

    \(W_1:768\!\to\!3072\) and \(W_2:3072\!\to\!768\); \(\alpha\) is initialised at \(0.1\) and grows to \(0.1413\). \((\mu_{\mathrm{src}},\sigma_{\mathrm{src}})\) are frozen MuRIL statistics measured on the training split; \((\gamma,\beta)\) are initialised at T5's measured per-channel statistics and then trained.

    The backbone's text projection is an identity, and we add no trainable projection in its place. Replacing the encoder is therefore not a question of matching dimensionality: the substitute must approximate the T5 context geometry — token norm and inter-token correlation — that the frozen model expects. Subtracting the corpus mean before recolouring into T5 statistics restores 13.8× more caption-specific signal than an output layer normalisation, which cannot remove a direction shared across every token.

    A geometry gate runs before the first optimisation step: it measures the adapter's output on 128 held-out captions and refuses to start training unless that distribution matches T5's. The reported run passed on the first attempt, at token norm 7.123, inter-caption cosine 0.7501 and between-to-mean ratio 0.5719, against T5 references of 6.758, 0.6563 and 0.7201.

    Motion as the pivot

    Two language views \(a\) and \(b\) of the same motion window are driven toward the same prediction, with real motion as the primary target and a weak frozen-English response as an anchor:

    \[ \mathcal{L}=\tfrac{1}{2}\big[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nat}}(a)+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nat}}(b)\big] +\lambda_x\big\|\hat{x}_0(a)-\hat{x}_0(b)\big\|^2 +\tfrac{\lambda_{\mathrm{en}}}{2}\Big[\big\|\hat{x}_0(a)-\mathrm{sg}\,\hat{x}_0^{\,\mathrm{en}}\big\|^2 +\big\|\hat{x}_0(b)-\mathrm{sg}\,\hat{x}_0^{\,\mathrm{en}}\big\|^2\Big] \]

    \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nat}}\) is the backbone's own denoising loss against the real motion; \(\mathrm{sg}\) is a stop-gradient. We anneal \(\lambda_x\) from \(0.5\) to \(0.2\) and hold \(\lambda_{\mathrm{en}}=0.1\).

    Both branches replay one saved random state, so the noise level, the noise, the noised input and the adapter's dropout mask are identical — the only difference between them is which caption produced the context. Because real motion carries weight one, the English response is a weak auxiliary teacher rather than a ceiling, and it is applied symmetrically to both Indic branches, so no Indic language is privileged. Text-induced variance, monitored but never optimised, rose from \(2.0\times10^{-4}\) to \(2.5\text{–}4.2\times10^{-3}\) over training.

    04 — Results

    87–91% of the stock-English control, from a caption the model could not read

    Evaluation uses 1,024 test windows, one per super-group, drawn from a test split rebuilt so that 0% of it was seen during backbone pretraining — against 56.5% for a conventionally built split. All arms share windows, 10-frame histories and identifier-hashed noise, so every comparison is paired item by item. Chance R@1 is 0.031.

    Single-word instruction following

    One word in the Indic caption is swapped for its opposite and the motion regenerated with matched history and noise. Ground truth is a kinematic quantity — metres of sagittal displacement, or metres per second of pelvis speed — not a rater. The null baseline is 50%.

    Forward and backward use 256 pairs per language; speed uses 95–138. Stock ties at the null because a swapped Indic word changes nothing its tokenizer can see, which is what gives the measure its range.

    05 — Renders

    Eight clips, four languages, one shared adapter

    Every render shares a clip, a checkpoint, guidance 2.5 and seed 0 — only the language changes. Each video has four panels: ground truth, stock + English, stock + Indic, and ours + Indic. Generation is closed-loop over 140–290 frames, with each 60-frame chunk fed back as history. Across the 32 clip–language pairs, ours has lower pose error on 25.
